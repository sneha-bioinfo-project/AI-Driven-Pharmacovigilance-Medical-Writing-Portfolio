The associated narrative review manuscript, titled **"Machine Learning for Early Detection of Adverse Drug Reactions Using Big Data from Electronic Health Records"**, explores the application of machine learning approaches for improving adverse drug reaction (ADR) surveillance using healthcare data.

Adverse drug reactions represent a significant global healthcare challenge, contributing to patient harm, increased healthcare burden, and limitations in traditional pharmacovigilance workflows. Conventional spontaneous reporting systems are affected by under-reporting and delayed signal detection. This review examines how machine learning applied to Electronic Health Records (EHRs) can support earlier and more proactive ADR identification.

## Key Highlights of the Manuscript

- Evaluation of machine learning applications for ADR prediction using structured and unstructured healthcare data.

- Review of predictive approaches including Random Forest, XGBoost, Support Vector Machines, and deep learning architectures.

- Analysis of benchmark datasets and pharmacovigilance resources, including MIMIC-III, SIDER, FAERS, and PvPI.

- Discussion of essential data engineering workflows, including data preprocessing, vocabulary standardisation (RxNorm and MedDRA), and management of class imbalance.

- Exploration of future implementation strategies involving Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) and privacy-preserving Federated Learning frameworks.

The manuscript also discusses opportunities and challenges associated with implementing AI-driven pharmacovigilance systems within evolving digital health ecosystems, including initiatives such as India's Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM).
