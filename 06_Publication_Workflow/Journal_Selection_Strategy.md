# Journal Selection Strategy & Evaluation Framework

**Educational Portfolio Material – Publication Management Demonstration**  
**Topic Focus:** Machine Learning and Natural Language Processing in Pharmacovigilance  
**Document Purpose:** Demonstrates standard medical writing processes for evaluating, scoring, and selecting target journals prior to manuscript submission.

---

## 1. Objective and Evaluation Criteria
Selecting an appropriate target journal is critical for ensuring research visibility, appropriate peer review, and alignment with target stakeholder groups (pharmacovigilance specialists, regulatory scientists, and clinical researchers). 

Medical writers evaluate candidate journals based on six primary parameters:
1. **Scope Alignment:** Match between manuscript subject matter and journal aims.
2. **Target Audience:** Reach among drug safety professionals, biostatisticians, and regulatory bodies.
3. **Indexing & Discoverability:** Inclusions in MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science.
4. **Article Type Specifications:** Acceptance of narrative reviews, word count limits, and reference constraints.
5. **Editorial Metrics:** Average time to first decision, peer review rigor, and acceptance rate.
6. **Publishing Model & Licensing:** Open Access (OA) availability, Article Processing Charges (APCs), and copyright retention.

## 4. Strategic Recommendation & Submission Hierarchy

### Primary Choice: *Drug Safety*
* **Rationale:** As the flagship journal of the International Society of Pharmacovigilance (ISoP), *Drug Safety* directly targets decision-makers in post-marketing surveillance and regulatory science. The review's emphasis on balancing automated NLP extraction with human-in-the-loop governance aligns with the journal's focus on methodological rigor and regulatory applicability.
* **Action Plan:** Format manuscript strictly according to Adis/Springer author guidelines (Vancouver citation style, structured abstract under 250 words, key points summary box).

### Contingency Plan (Secondary Target): *Expert Opinion on Drug Safety*
* **Rationale:** If *Drug Safety* declines review or requests substantial scope refocusing, *Expert Opinion on Drug Safety* represents an optimal secondary choice. It places strong emphasis on expert interpretation of emerging methodologies, providing a suitable platform for highlighting implementation challenges of machine learning tools.
