# Abstract

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) represent a critical global healthcare challenge, contributing substantially to patient morbidity, mortality, and healthcare expenditure.

Traditional pharmacovigilance approaches, including spontaneous reporting systems, are limited by under-reporting and delayed signal detection, particularly in resource-constrained settings such as India.

Machine learning (ML) applied to electronic health records (EHRs) offers transformative potential for early and accurate ADR detection by leveraging both structured data, including demographics, laboratory results, and prescriptions, and unstructured data such as clinical notes.

State-of-the-art ML methodologies, including tree-based algorithms, deep learning architectures, and natural language processing techniques, have demonstrated improved ADR signal detection across diverse datasets such as MIMIC-III, FAERS, SIDER, and PvPI.

Key challenges in India's evolving digital health ecosystem, including data imbalance, data heterogeneity, and limited interoperability, are discussed. A comprehensive methodological framework incorporating data preprocessing, feature engineering, imbalance correction approaches such as SMOTE, and model evaluation metrics including ROC-AUC and F1-score is proposed for developing robust ADR prediction pipelines.

Integration of ML-driven pharmacovigilance with initiatives such as the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) has the potential to enhance patient safety and support a transition from reactive to proactive ADR monitoring through explainable, federated, and scalable solutions.
